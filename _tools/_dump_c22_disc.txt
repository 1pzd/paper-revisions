196: 
197: This study has four concrete limitations. First, the cohort contains 1,673 single-center images, so device-specific and population-specific biases cannot be excluded. Second, the long-tailed distribution leaves the two most severe grades with only 4 and 3 test images, so their per-grade estimates carry wide confidence intervals despite the bounded-error behavior. Third, although labels follow LOCS III consensus grading by three senior ophthalmologists, comorbidities (for example, corneal opacity) may still confound appearance-based grading, and external multi-center validation remains outstanding. Fourth, the baseline comparison does not include medical vision-language models such as BiomedCLIP, MedCLIP, and PubMedCLIP. These models are pretrained on radiology, pathology, or biomedical figure-caption corpora rather than slit-lamp anterior-segment photography, so zero-shot transfer to LOCS III grading would conflate imaging-modality mismatch with representation quality. A fair comparison would require fine-tuning each backbone under the same long-tailed protocol, which is beyond the scope of the present limited-data study; our zero-shot CLIP and linear-probe baselines instead isolate the contribution of vision-language pretraining and supervised adaptation within the target modality. We leave the comparison with domain-specific medical vision-language backbones to future multi-center work.

199: 5 Discussion
200: 
201: This study presents CLIP-CAT, a vision-language framework designed to address the challenges of data scarcity, class imbalance, and ordinality in cataract grading. By combining adaptive patching for micro-lesion detection with the Bidirectional Rank-Aware Loss, our approach outperforms generic backbones and task-specific baselines while offering a language-aligned representation relevant to future decision-support research.
202: 
203: The fusion of global and local visual streams enables the model to capture both anatomical context and fine-grained lesion patterns. The global CLIP encoder provides semantic alignment with medical text descriptions, while the entropy-driven local patching mechanism prioritizes regions with high texture heterogeneity that correspond to lens opacities. This dual-stream architecture mirrors the clinical examination workflow, where ophthalmologists first perform a global scan and then focus on suspicious areas.
204: 
205: The Bidirectional Rank-Aware Loss enforces the ordinal structure of disease severity, reducing catastrophic cross-grade errors. The low mean squared error (0.42) and the concentration of errors on adjacent grades (90.9%) demonstrate that the model has learned the continuous progression of cataract severity rather than treating grades as independent categories.
206: 
207: The medical prompt engineering mechanism leverages the pretrained CLIP semantic space to inject clinical priors without requiring large-scale annotated data. By describing each grade with LOCS III-based clinical terminology, the text embeddings serve as semantic anchors that guide the visual feature learning. The ablation study confirms that replacing these medical descriptions with learnable class tokens reduces performance, indicating that the benefit stems from the clinical semantics encoded in the prompts rather than merely adding parameters.
208: 
209: Although the current study is limited by a relatively small, single-center dataset and potential confounders from comorbidities, five-fold cross-validation indicates stable performance across data splits. The tight variance (86.1% ± 0.85% accuracy) suggests that the architectural priors and regularization strategies effectively prevent overfitting.
210: 
211: Future work will prioritize multi-center external validation to assess generalization across different imaging devices and patient populations. Refining prompt engineering with more detailed clinical descriptions and exploring mixup-style augmentation (46) may further improve robustness to imaging artifacts and complex ocular conditions in future experiments.

213: 6 Conclusion
214: 
215: In conclusion, this study demonstrates that vision-language models can leverage medical text prompts and ordinal constraints for automated cataract grading on limited and imbalanced data. CLIP-CAT achieves 86.76% accuracy and 0.42 mean squared error, outperforming generic backbones and task-specific methods. The bounded error behavior and language-aligned representation are relevant to future decision-support research, but the system remains a research prototype requiring external, prospective, workflow-level validation. Multi-center external validation remains a priority for future work to confirm generalization across diverse clinical settings.
