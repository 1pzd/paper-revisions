--- A7 FULL ---
While cataracts are the primary cause of reversible blindness worldwide, current diagnostic workflows rely heavily on subjective manual grading[1,2]. This labor-intensive approach is increasingly unsustainable given the rising prevalence of the disease and the scarcity of ophthalmologists in developing regions. Consequently, developing objective Computer-Aided Diagnosis systems is no longer just an option but a necessity to standardize grading and prevent avoidable blindness.
--- A9 FULL ---
Despite the success of deep learning in binary tasks, fine-grained clinical grading remains challenging due to three limitations. First, standard classification losses treat ordinal disease stages as independent classes, ignoring the progressive nature of severity (e.g., Grade 0 < Grade 5). Second, the inherent long-tailed distribution of medical data biases models toward majority classes, compromising sensitivity for rare, severe cases. Third, most black box models rely on low-level feature statistics rather than semantic reasoning, lacking the clinical priors necessary for robust generalization and interpretability[7-10].
--- A15 FULL ---
Cataract grading has evolved from hand-crafted features to deep learning. CNN backbones [11,12] improve feature extraction but carry ImageNet domain bias, and Vision Transformers [13] risk overfitting on limited data. Task-specific systems incorporate clinical structure: CDRFF-Net fuses discriminative region features with ordinal regression [5], and a LOCS III-based hybrid model aligns outputs with the clinical scale [6], while rank-consistent and kappa-based objectives penalize cross-grade errors [14,15]. Nevertheless, most existing methods treat grading as nominal multi-class classification, overlooking the intrinsic ordinal nature of disease severity[4-6,16].
--- A17 FULL ---
Vision-language models such as CLIP [17] align images and text in a shared embedding space, and parameter-efficient tuning methods facilitate downstream adaptation [18]. Medical applications have grown in radiology and pathology, yet ophthalmic adoption remains limited [19,20]. In cataract assessment, deep grading systems built on clinical scales have been developed for slit-lamp and retro-illumination photographs [21], nuclear cataract diagnosis from slit-lamp video [22], and cortical cataract grading with clinical sub-scores [23], but these pipelines remain CNN- or Transformer-based classifiers without language-aligned semantics. Standard contrastive alignment, moreover, does not preserve the ordinal topology of cataract severity. Unlike prior pipelines that attach ordinal losses to CNN features [5,15], this study couples vision-language alignment with a Bidirectional Rank-Aware Loss and grade-specific medical prompts.
