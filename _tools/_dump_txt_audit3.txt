115: 3.5 Dataset Description and Ethics Statement
116: 
117: To validate our framework, we curated a private clinical dataset of 1,673 slit-lamp images from 1,673 distinct patients (one image per patient). The cohort included patients across all age stages and of both sexes. All images were collected via routine clinical slit-lamp photography; the specific slit-lamp models and acquisition parameters were not recorded. Ground truth was established by three senior ophthalmologists via consensus voting: each image was graded by all three graders, and the final label was determined by consensus. As the reference standard reflects consensus rather than independent parallel ratings, inter-rater κ is not reported. Each image carries both a cortical grade (C0 to C5) and a nuclear color grade (NC1 to NC6) on the LOCS III scale. This study targets joint severity grading, where each case is labeled with both a nuclear color grade (NC1 to NC6) and a cortical opacity grade (C0 to C5). The final severity level G ∈ {G0, G1, G2, G3, G4, G5} is computed as the average of the nuclear color grade and the cortical opacity grade, rounded up to the nearest integer: with NC and C encoded as integers from 0 to 5 (NC1 to NC6 as 0 to 5; C0 to C5 as 0 to 5), G = ⌈(NC + C)/2⌉. The dataset exhibits a severe long-tailed distribution (Figure 4): the two head grades (NC2 and NC3) comprise 78.1% of samples, the mildest grade (NC1) accounts for 13.7%, and the two most severe grades (NC5 and NC6) together cover only 2.1%. Without intervention, this imbalance risks biasing the model toward the majority class to minimize global loss, thereby failing to identify severe, sight-threatening cases.

215: In conclusion, this study demonstrates that vision-language models can leverage medical text prompts and ordinal constraints for automated cataract grading on limited and imbalanced data. CLIP-CAT achieves 86.76% accuracy and 0.42 mean squared error, outperforming generic backbones and task-specific methods. The bounded error behavior and language-aligned representation are relevant to future decision-support research, but the system remains a research prototype requiring external, prospective, workflow-level validation. Multi-center external validation remains a priority for future work to confirm generalization across diverse clinical settings.
