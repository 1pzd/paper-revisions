L115: 3.5 Dataset Description and Ethics Statement

L116: 

L117: To validate our framework, we curated a private clinical dataset of 1,673 slit-lamp images from 1,673 distinct patients (one image per patient). The cohort included patients across all age stages and of both sexes. All images were collected via routine clinical slit-lamp photography; the specific slit-lamp models and acquisition parameters were not recorded. Ground truth was established by three senior ophthalmologists via consensus voting: each image was graded by all three graders, and the final label was determined by consensus. As the reference standard reflects consensus rather than independent parallel ratings, inter-rater κ is not reported. Each image carries both a cortical grade (C0 to C5) and a nuclear color grade (NC1 to NC6) on the LOCS III scale. This study targets joint severity grading, where each case is labeled with both a nuclear color grade (NC1 to NC6) and a cortical opacity grade (C0 to C5). The final severity level G ∈ {G0, G1, G2, G3, G4, G5} is computed as the average of the nuclear color grade and the cortical opacity grade, rounded up to the nearest integer: with NC and C encoded as integers from 0 to 5 (NC1 to NC6 as 0 to 5; C0 to C5 as 0 to 5), G = ⌈(NC + C)/2⌉. The dataset exhibits a severe long-tailed distribution (Figure 4): the two head grades (NC2 and NC3) comprise 78.1% of samples, the mildest grade (NC1) accounts for 13.7%, and the two most severe grades (NC5 and NC6) together cover only 2.1%. Without intervention, this imbalance risks biasing the model toward the majority class to minimize global loss, thereby failing to identify severe, sight-threatening cases.

L118: 

L185: Among the 44 misclassified test images, 40 (90.9%) land on immediately adjacent grades, 4 (9.1%) deviate by two grades, and none deviate by three or more. This error distribution substantiates the bounded error property: the corresponding discrete-grade MSE (MSE_hard) computed from the confusion matrix is 0.17, calculated as (40×1² + 4×2²)/335 = 56/335 ≈ 0.167. The expected-grade MSE (MSE_exp = 0.42, reported in Table 1) is higher because it measures the squared distance between the ground truth and the probabilistic expectation Σ_g g·p(g|x), which accounts for probability mass distributed across multiple grades rather than a single hard prediction. Both metrics confirm that CLIP-CAT's predictions remain concentrated near the true grade. Errors lean toward over-grading (27 versus 17), so the clinically riskier direction, under-grading of Grades 4 to 5, occurs in only 2 test images (0.6%), and every such deviation remains within one grade. Confusions concentrate between neighboring mid-grades (for example, G2↔G3, 13 cases: 10 + 3), which matches the continuous progression of nuclear opacity and explains why the rank-aware term remains effective. L186: 

L195: All results reported in this work are obtained on one institution's slit-lamp images. A multi-center validation protocol is under preparation: the trained model will be applied without fine-tuning to cohorts collected from other centers and grading workflows, and any performance drift will be analyzed with the same per-grade and confusion-matrix methodology. External validation results are not yet available, and we restrict our claims accordingly.

L196: 

L197: This study has four concrete limitations. First, the cohort contains 1,673 single-center images, so device-specific and population-specific biases cannot be excluded. Second, the long-tailed distribution leaves the two most severe grades with only 4 and 3 test images, so their per-grade estimates carry wide confidence intervals despite the bounded-error behavior. Third, although labels follow LOCS III consensus grading by three senior ophthalmologists, comorbidities (for example, corneal opacity) may still confound appearance-based grading, and external multi-center validation remains outstanding. Fourth, the baseline comparison does not include medical vision-language models such as BiomedCLIP, MedCLIP, and PubMedCLIP. These models are pretrained on radiology, pathology, or biomedical figure-caption corpora rather than slit-lamp anterior-segment photography, so zero-shot transfer to LOCS III grading would conflate imaging-modality mismatch with representation quality. A fair comparison would require fine-tuning each backbone under the same long-tailed protocol, which is beyond the scope of the present limited-data study; our zero-shot CLIP and linear-probe baselines instead isolate the contribution of vision-language pretraining and supervised adaptation within the target modality. We leave the comparison with domain-specific medical vision-language backbones to future multi-center work.
