Cataract, a clouding of the eye's natural lens, is the world's leading cause of avoidable blindness. Grading how severe the clouding is helps doctors choose the right moment for surgery. In practice, trained specialists grade eye photographs by comparing them with a standard chart—a process that is slow and differs between observers. Computer-based grading could help, but it normally needs very large sets of expert-labelled photographs that hospitals rarely have, especially for rare, severe cases.

This study presents CLIP-CAT, a computer system that grades cataract severity from clinical eye photographs. It combines image analysis with written descriptions of each grade, so it learns effectively from limited data. It also treats grading as an ordered scale and punishes large mistakes more than small ones. On 1,673 photographs from one hospital in China, the system reached 86.76% accuracy, and nearly all of its errors were neighbouring grades rather than serious misjudgements.

This work has a methodological scope: it demonstrates a practical recipe for building a reliable grading model without massive data. The system remains a research prototype—not a medical device—and multi-centre clinical validation is still needed before any clinical use. All code is released publicly to support future research.
