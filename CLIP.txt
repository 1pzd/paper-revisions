CLIP-CAT: Vision-Language Fusion Framework for Ordinal Cataract Grading on Limited and Imbalanced Data
Shuang Wang1 ,Zhida Pang1
1 School of Computer Science and Engineering, Northeastern University, Shenyang, China
Abstract. Cataract is the leading cause of reversible blindness worldwide, yet automated grading remains challenging due to limited labeled data, severe class imbalance, and the inherently ordinal progression of the disease. Existing CNN-based approaches commonly treat cataract grading as a nominal classification task, overlooking the ordinal disease structure and under-utilizing medical prior knowledge. To address these challenges, we propose CLIP-CAT, a vision-language model that leverages medical text prompts to alleviate data scarcity and improve grading performance. Our approach combines language-guided global semantic representations and localized fine-grained feature learning in a unified framework. To mitigate the limitations of global encoders in detecting subtle lesions, we introduce Adaptive Patching for enhanced local representation. In addition, we design a Bidirectional Rank-Aware Loss that encourages unimodal probability distributions, effectively modeling the inherent ordinality of the task. Experiments on 1,673 slit-lamp images demonstrate that CLIP-CAT achieves 86.76% accuracy and 0.42 MSE, outperforming generic backbones and task-specific grading methods, with per-grade analysis and five-fold cross-validation confirming that errors concentrate on adjacent grades and that performance is stable.
Keywords: Cataract Grading, Vision-Language Models, Limited-Scale Learning
1Introduction
While cataracts are the primary cause of reversible blindness worldwide, current diagnostic workflows rely heavily on subjective manual grading[1,2]. This labor-intensive approach is increasingly unsustainable given the rising prevalence of the disease and the scarcity of ophthalmologists in developing regions. Consequently, developing objective Computer-Aided Diagnosis systems is no longer just an option but a necessity to standardize grading and prevent avoidable blindness.
Clinical Motivation and Imaging Choice. Slit-lamp imaging visualizes lens opacity directly and underpins the LOCS III grading standard [3], whereas fundus photography and OCT angiography primarily characterize retinal and choroidal structures and offer only indirect evidence of lens status. Automated cataract grading systems are therefore typically developed on slit-lamp or anterior-segment images [4-6], and we target this modality to stay consistent with clinical practice.
Despite the success of deep learning in binary tasks, fine-grained clinical grading remains challenging due to three limitations. First, standard classification losses treat ordinal disease stages as independent classes, ignoring the progressive nature of severity (e.g., Grade 0 < Grade 5). Second, the inherent long-tailed distribution of medical data biases models toward majority classes, compromising sensitivity for rare, severe cases. Third, most black box models rely on low-level feature statistics rather than semantic reasoning, lacking the clinical priors necessary for robust generalization and interpretability[7-10].
To address these challenges, we propose CLIP-CAT, a vision-language framework that mimics the clinical diagnostic workflow. The main contributions of this study are three-fold:
1.A Multi-Granularity Vision-Language Framework: We introduce a unified framework that fuses global semantic anchors with local adaptive patching. This approach mimics the global-scan, local-focus strategy of human experts, enhancing the model's ability to detect subtle lesions missed by traditional encoders.
2.Ordinal-Aware Optimization Objective: We propose a Bidirectional Rank-Aware Constraint that redefines the classification loss. By enforcing unimodal probability distributions and penalizing distance-based errors, we ensure predictions respect the strict logical progression of disease severity (e.g., Grade 0 < Grade 5).
3.Robustness to Long-Tailed Distributions: We design a Prototype Smoothing strategy that maintains momentum-updated global class centers (Section 4.2) and combines it with prompt tuning. This stabilizes training on small, imbalanced clinical datasets, where minority grades are under-represented.
2Related Work
2.1Deep Learning for Cataract Grading
Cataract grading has evolved from hand-crafted features to deep learning. CNN backbones [11,12] improve feature extraction but carry ImageNet domain bias, and Vision Transformers [13] risk overfitting on limited data. Task-specific systems incorporate clinical structure: CDRF-Net fuses discriminative region features with ordinal regression [5], and a LOCS III-based hybrid model aligns outputs with the clinical scale [6], while rank-consistent and kappa-based objectives penalize cross-grade errors [14,15]. Nevertheless, most existing methods treat grading as nominal multi-class classification, overlooking the intrinsic ordinal nature of disease severity[4-6,16].
2.2Vision-Language Models in Medicine
Vision-language models such as CLIP [17] align images and text in a shared embedding space, and parameter-efficient tuning methods facilitate downstream adaptation [18]. Medical applications have grown in radiology and pathology, yet ophthalmic adoption remains limited [19,20]. Standard contrastive alignment, moreover, does not preserve the ordinal topology of cataract severity. Unlike prior pipelines that attach ordinal losses to CNN features [5,15], this study couples vision-language alignment with a bidirectional rank-aware objective and grade-specific medical prompts.
3Methodology
3.1Framework Overview and Problem Definition
As illustrated in Fig. 1, the proposed CLIP-CAT framework is designed to mimic the cognitive workflow of clinical ophthalmologists: Global Assessment  Local Refinement  Semantic Reasoning. The architecture consists of three synergistic components:
岸Dual-Stream Visual Encoder: Integrating a global CLIP-based branch to capture anatomical structure with a local Adaptive Patching branch to isolate subtle lesion details. These streams are fused dynamically via an attention mechanism.
岸Medical Prompt Engineering: Unlike traditional one-hot labels, we utilize expert clinical descriptions as semantic anchors. This projects visual features into a language-aligned space, enabling the model to understand medical severity.
岸Bidirectional Rank-Aware Optimization: To address the ordinal nature of cataracts, we introduce a specialized loss function that penalizes predictions based on their distance from the true grade, enforcing a unimodal probability distribution.
Given a slit-lamp image x, the model predicts the cataract severity grade G ﹋ {G0, G1, G2, G3, G4, G5}, where the severity is computed as the weighted sum of the nuclear color grade (NC, ranging from NC1 to NC6) and the posterior subcapsular opacity grade (P, ranging from P0 to P5), both following the LOCS III standard [13]. In this study, NC and P contribute equally (weight = 1) to determine the final severity. Higher grades indicate more severe cataracts.
Our objective is to predict a probability distribution  that not only maximizes accuracy but also respects the ordinal topology of the disease. Specifically, the model must minimize the expectation of the ordinal distance loss , ensuring that severe cross-grade errors (e.g., confusing Grade 0 with Grade 4) are penalized more heavily than adjacent errors[14].

3.3Medical Prompt Engineering Mechanism
Unlike traditional -way classifiers that map visual features to semantically meaningless one-hot vectors, CLIP-CAT injects expert medical priors via natural language. This transforms the classification task into a vision-language alignment problem.
Prompts as Semantic Supervision, Not Label Rewriting. A label paraphrase would merely restate the class index in words and, once encoded, would remain dominated by surface form. Our templates instead describe the clinical appearance of each grade〞opacity density, color, and spatial pattern〞drawn from the LOCS III vocabulary [3]. The pre-trained text encoder maps these descriptions into a semantic space whose geometry reflects clinical similarity, so adjacent grades receive nearby anchors while distant grades are well separated. The model is therefore supervised by medical semantics rather than arbitrary class identifiers.

4Experiments
4.1Dataset Description and Long-tail Analysis
To validate our framework, we curated a private clinical dataset of 1,673 slit-lamp images, with ground truth established by three senior ophthalmologists via consensus voting. Each image carries both a cortical grade (C0每C5) and a severity grade (NC1每NC6) on the LOCS III scale; this study targets joint severity grading, where each case is labeled with both a nuclear color grade (NC1每NC6) and a posterior subcapsular opacity grade (P0每P5). The final severity level G ﹋ {G0, G1, G2, G3, G4, G5} is computed as the weighted sum of NC and P (with equal weights). The distribution of the 1,673 cases across the six severity grades is, while the cortical labels (5/302/446/445/325/150 images over C0每C5) are reserved for future multi-task extension. The dataset exhibits a severe Long-tailed Distribution: the two head grades (NC2 and NC3) comprise 78.1% of samples, the mildest grade (NC1) accounts for 13.7%, and the two most severe grades (NC5 and NC6) together cover only 2.1%. Without intervention, this imbalance risks biasing the model toward the majority class (NC2) to minimize global loss, thereby failing to identify severe, sight-threatening cases.

4.2Implementation Details
Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 4050 using PyTorch 1.12. We used the AdamW optimizer and a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule (initial )[22]. Due to VRAM limits, the batch size was set to 8. To mitigate instability from small batches, we implemented momentum updates for global class centers. Focal Loss  was employed to suppress gradients from easy Grade 0 samples.
All slit-lamp images were resized to 224 ℅ 224 before entering the encoders. The global branch partially fine-tunes the CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder〞the first 12 transformer blocks are frozen while the remaining 12 blocks, the attention fusion layer, and the projection layers are trained〞and the text encoder provides a fixed pretrained semantic space remains frozen to preserve the pretrained CLIP semantic space. The dataset was split into training, validation and test subsets in a 7:1:2 ratio (1171/167/335 images); models were trained for 50 epochs with weight decay 0.01, and the checkpoint with the best validation performance was used for evaluation. Random horizontal flipping and mild color jitter were applied as data augmentation. The momentum coefficient of the prototype smoothing (global class center) update was set to 0.9. The temperature 而 of the semantic projection was initialized to 0.07 and learned end-to-end; the loss weight 竹 was 1.0; and K = 6 patches were retained per image. All results are averaged over 3 independent runs with different random seeds (42, 123, 2024) runs.
4.3Results Analysis: Comparison with Baseline and Task-Specific Methods
We benchmarked our CLIP-CAT against generic CNN backbones (ResNet50, DenseNet121), a Transformer backbone (ViT-B/16), CLIP transfer baselines (zero-shot and linear probing), an ordinal regression baseline (ResNet50 + CORAL), and two task-specific cataract grading methods (CDRF-Net and a LOCS III-based hybrid). The quantitative results are summarized in Table 2[5,6,11-13,15,17].
Performance Superiority: CLIP-CAT outperforms the strongest baseline (CDRF-Net, 83.10%) by 3.66% in accuracy (86.76% vs. 83.10%) and exceeds the best generic backbone (ViT-B/16, 82.55%) by 4.21%. While CNN architectures like ResNet50 provide a reasonable baseline (78.45%), their relatively high Mean Squared Error (MSE = 1.24) suggests they struggle to capture the subtle, progressive nature of cataract severity.
MSE Reduction & Clinical Safety: A critical contribution of our method is the reduction in MSE to 0.42 (computed on the expected predicted grade). In the context of ordinal regression, MSE reflects the distance of the error. An MSE of 0.42 implies an average prediction deviation of  grades. This indicates a Bounded Error property: even when CLIP-CAT misclassifies a sample, the prediction typically lands on the immediate neighboring grade, rather than making catastrophic errors. This property is vital for clinical safety, ensuring that the model's decision logic aligns with the continuous physiological progression of the disease. Furthermore, the high Kappa coefficient (0.88) confirms strong consistency with human expert grading[14].
Transfer and Ordinal Baselines: Applying CLIP [17] in a zero-shot manner yields only 64.20% accuracy, and a linear probe on frozen CLIP features reaches 79.50%, indicating that the vision-language prior alone is insufficient for fine-grained grading. Equipping ResNet50 with the CORAL ordinal objective [15] raises accuracy to 80.30% and reduces MSE from 1.24 to 0.90, confirming the benefit of ordinal modeling, yet it remains well below CLIP-CAT. Among task-specific methods, CDRF-Net [5] and the LOCS III-based hybrid model [6] achieve 83.10% and 82.65%, respectively. Both incorporate domain priors and ordinal structure, so the remaining 3.66% accuracy advantage and lower MSE (0.42 vs. 0.72) of CLIP-CAT suggest that the joint global-local evidence aggregation and bidirectional rank-aware training provide complementary gains over existing pipelines.

4.4Ablation Study
To verify the contribution of each component in our framework, we conducted a step-by-step ablation study. The results, detailed in Table 3, demonstrate the additive value of each module:
岸Baseline (Row 1): The naive application of CLIP yields an accuracy of 80.15%. While promising, the domain gap between natural images and slit-lamp photography limits its precision.
岸Impact of Local Patches (Row 2): Incorporating the Adaptive Patching Local Branch improves accuracy to 82.40%. This confirms that fine-grained local features are essential for capturing early-stage lesions that are often lost in global semantic tokens.
岸Impact of Rank Loss (Row 3): The integration of Rank Loss provides the most significant improvement in error control, reducing the MSE from 0.92 to 0.55. This validates that explicitly penalizing non-ordinal predictions forces the model to learn the topological order of severity, effectively eliminating large-span outliers.
岸Impact of Imbalance Strategy (Row 4): Finally, combining the weighted sampling and Focal Loss strategies addresses the long-tail issue, further boosting the accuracy to 86.76% and achieving the lowest MSE of 0.42.
岸Impact of Entropy-Based Patch Selection (Rows 5每6): Replacing entropy-based top-K selection with random patch sampling lowers accuracy from 82.40% to 81.30% in the two-module setting and from 86.76% to 85.92% in the full model, while slightly increasing MSE. The gain of the local branch therefore stems from lesion-oriented patch prioritization rather than extra parameters. The entropy statistic is also clinically grounded: early cortical and nuclear opacities elevate local intensity heterogeneity, so high-entropy patches concentrate on lesion-rich regions without manual annotation.

Among the 44 misclassified test images, 40 (90.9%) land on immediately adjacent grades, 4 (9.1%) deviate by two grades, and none deviate by three or more, substantiating the Bounded Error property claimed in Section 4.3 and keeping the expected-grade MSE at 0.42. 4.6Generalizability and Limitations
Single-center datasets with severe long tails pose a genuine risk of overfitting, and the absolute scale of our cohort (1,673 images) is modest by contemporary standards. We therefore examine the stability of the reported results under resampling, summarize the regularization measures adopted during training, and state the remaining limitations explicitly.
To quantify split sensitivity, we repeated the evaluation with 5-fold cross-validation over the full dataset: CLIP-CAT attains 86.1% ㊣ 0.85% accuracy, an MSE of 0.45 ㊣ 0.05, and a Kappa of 0.87 ㊣ 0.02 across folds. The mean accuracy closely tracks the single-split result of 86.76%, and the small standard deviation indicates that the performance does not depend on a favorable train/test partition.
Several mechanisms constrain overfitting on this data scale. Only the upper 12 transformer blocks of the CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder, the attention fusion layer, and the projection layers are trainable, while the first 12 blocks and the text encoder remain fixed, reducing the number of parameters that must be learned from 1,400 training images. Training further applies random horizontal flipping and mild color jitter, weight decay, and momentum-based prototype smoothing (0.9) for stable class centers; the checkpoint with the best validation performance is selected for evaluation. The tight cross-validation variance above and the consistent ranking of modules in Table 3 suggest that the gain stems from architectural priors rather than dataset-specific memorization.
External Validation Plan. All results reported in this work are obtained on one institution＊s slit-lamp images. A multi-center validation protocol is under preparation: the trained model will be applied without fine-tuning to cohorts collected from other centers and grading workflows, and any performance drift will be analyzed with the same per-grade and confusion-matrix methodology of Section 4.5. External validation results are not yet available, and we restrict our claims accordingly.
Limitations. This study has three concrete limitations. First, the cohort contains 1,673 single-center images, so device- and population-specific biases cannot be excluded. Second, the long-tailed distribution leaves the two most severe grades with only 4 and 3 test images (20 and 15 images in the full cohort), so their per-grade estimates carry wide confidence intervals despite the bounded-error behavior observed in Section 4.5. Third, although labels follow LOCS III consensus grading by three senior ophthalmologists, comorbidities (e.g., corneal opacity) may still confound appearance-based grading, and external multi-center validation remains outstanding.
5Conclusion
In conclusion, this study presents CLIP-CAT, a vision-language framework designed to address the challenges of data scarcity, class imbalance, and ordinality in cataract grading. By combining adaptive patching for micro-lesion detection with a bidirectional rank-aware loss, our approach outperforms generic backbones and task-specific baselines while offering interpretable, semantic reasoning for clinical use. Although the current study is limited by a relatively small, single-center dataset and potential confounders from comorbidities, five-fold cross-validation indicates stable performance and we restrict our claims accordingly. Future work will prioritize multi-center external validation, refining prompt engineering, and exploring mixup-style augmentation[23] to enhance robustness against imaging artifacts and complex ocular conditions in real-world scenarios.
