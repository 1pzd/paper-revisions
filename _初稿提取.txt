
CLIP-CAT: Vision-Language Fusion Framework for Ordinal Cataract Grading on Few-Shot Data`r`nShuang Wang1 ,Zhida Pang1`r`n1 School of Computer Science and Engineering, Northeastern University, Shenyang, China`r`nAbstract. Cataract is the leading cause of reversible blindness worldwide, yet automated grading remains challenging due to limited labeled data, severe class imbalance, and the inherently ordinal progression of the disease. Existing CNN-based approaches commonly treat cataract grading as a nominal classification task, overlooking the ordinal disease structure and under-utilizing medical prior knowledge. To address these challenges, we propose CLIP-CAT, a vision language modal that leverages medical text prompts to alleviate data scarcity and improve grading performance. Our approach combines language-guided global semantic representations and localized fine-grained feature learning in a unified framework. To mitigate the limitations of global encoders in detecting subtle lesions, we introduce Adaptive Patching for enhanced local representation. In addition, we design a Bidirectional Rank-Aware Loss that encourages unimodal probability distributions, effectively modeling the inherent ordinality of the task. Experiments on 2,000 images demonstrate that CLIP-CAT achieves 86.76% accuracy and 0.42 MSE, significantly outperforming baselines by aligning with human diagnostic logic.`r`nKeywords: Cataract Grading, Vision-Language Models, Few-Shot Learning`r`nIntroduction`r`nWhile cataracts are the primary cause of reversible blindness worldwide, current diagnostic workflows rely heavily on subjective manual grading[1,2]. This labor-intensive approach is increasingly unsustainable given the rising prevalence of the disease and the scarcity of ophthalmologists in developing regions. Consequently, developing objective Computer-Aided Diagnosis systems is no longer just an option but a necessity to standardize grading and prevent avoidable blindness.`r`nDespite the success of deep learning in binary tasks, fine-grained clinical grading remains challenging due to three limitations. First, standard classification losses treat ordinal disease stages as independent classes, ignoring the progressive nature of severity (e.g., Grade 0 < Grade 5). Second, the inherent long-tailed distribution of medical data biases models toward majority classes, compromising sensitivity for rare, severe cases. Third, most black box models rely on low-level feature statistics rather than semantic reasoning, lacking the clinical priors necessary for robust generalization and interpretability[3-6].`r`nTo address these challenges, we propose CLIP-CAT, a vision-language framework that mimics the clinical diagnostic workflow. The main contributions of this study are three-fold:`r`nA Multi-Granularity Vision-Language Framework: We introduce a novel synergy mechanism that fuses global semantic anchors with local adaptive patching. This approach mimics the global-scan, local-focus strategy of human experts, enhancing the model's ability to detect subtle lesions missed by traditional encoders.`r`nOrdinal-Aware Optimization Objective: We propose a Bidirectional Rank-Aware Constraint that redefines the classification loss. By enforcing unimodal probability distributions and penalizing distance-based errors, we ensure predictions respect the strict logical progression of disease severity (e.g., Grade 0 < Grade 5).`r`nRobustness to Long-Tailed Distributions: We design a Prototype Smoothing Initialization strategy combined with prompt tuning. This enables the model to generalize effectively on small, imbalanced clinical datasets, significantly outperforming standard CNN architectures in few-shot scenarios.`r`nRelated Work`r`nDeep Learning for Cataract Grading`r`nCataract grading evolved from hand-crafted features to deep learning. While CNNs improved feature extraction, they introduce ImageNet-induced domain bias, and Vision Transformers struggle with overfitting in limited-data regimes. Crucially, most existing methods treat grading as nominal multi-class classification, overlooking the intrinsic ordinal nature of disease severity[7-10].`r`nVision-Language Models in Medicine`r`nVLMs like CLIP represent a shift to multimodal alignment, with efficient tuning methods facilitating downstream adaptation. Despite successes in radiology, ophthalmic applications are scarce. Crucially, standard contrastive losses lack the capacity to preserve the intrinsic ordinal topology of cataract severity. This study bridges this gap by integrating ordinal logic into vision-language alignment[11,12].`r`nMethodology`r`nProblem Definition and Notation`r`nAs illustrated in Fig. 1, the proposed CLIP-CAT framework is designed to mimic the cognitive workflow of clinical ophthalmologists: Global Assessment → Local Refinement → Semantic Reasoning. The architecture consists of three synergistic components:`r`nDual-Stream Visual Encoder: Integrating a global CLIP-based branch to capture anatomical structure with a local Adaptive Patching branch to isolate subtle lesion details. These streams are fused dynamically via an attention mechanism.`r`nMedical Prompt Engineering: Unlike traditional one-hot labels, we utilize expert clinical descriptions as semantic anchors. This projects visual features into a language-aligned space, enabling the model to understand medical severity.`r`nBidirectional Rank-Aware Optimization: To address the ordinal nature of cataracts, we introduce a specialized loss function that penalizes predictions based on their distance from the true grade, enforcing a unimodal probability distribution.`r`nFormally, we define the ordinal cataract grading task as learning a mapping function ℱ:X→Y. Let the training dataset be denoted as D={xi,yi}i=1N, where xi∈RH×W×3 represents the input slit-lamp image and yi∈{0,1,…,C−1} represents the ground truth severity label. Here, C=6 corresponds to the LOCS III grading scale[13].`r`nOur objective is to predict a probability distribution pyx that not only maximizes accuracy but also respects the ordinal topology of the disease. Specifically, the model must minimize the expectation of the ordinal distance loss ℒdisty,y, ensuring that severe cross-grade errors (e.g., confusing Grade 0 with Grade 4) are penalized more heavily than adjacent errors[14].`r`nVisual Encoder`r`nCLIP-CAT fuses global semantics with local details via a dual-stream visual encoder.`r`n`r`nFig.  SEQ "Figure" \* MERGEFORMAT 1. The dataset exhibits a severe Long-tailed Distribution: head classes comprise approximately 70% of samples, while tail classes are scarce. Without intervention, this imbalance risks biasing the model toward the majority class (Grade 0) to minimize global loss, thereby failing to identify severe, sight-threatening cases.`r`nImplementation Details`r`nExperiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 4050 using PyTorch 1.12. We used the AdamW optimizer and a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule (initial lr=1e−4)[15]. Due to VRAM limits, the batch size was set to 8. To mitigate instability from small batches, we implemented momentum updates for global class centers. Focal Loss γ=2.0 was employed to suppress gradients from easy Grade 0 samples.`r`nResults Analysis: CLIP-CAT vs. ResNet`r`nWe benchmarked our CLIP-CAT against standard CNN-based (ResNet50, DenseNet121) and Transformer-based (ViT-B/16) architectures. The quantitative results are summarized in Table 2[16-18].`r`nPerformance Superiority: CLIP-CAT outperforms the strongest baseline (ViT-B/16) by a significant margin of 4.21% in accuracy (86.76% vs. 82.55%). While CNN architectures like ResNet50 provide a reasonable baseline (78.45%), their relatively high Mean Squared Error (MSE = 1.24) suggests they struggle to capture the subtle, progressive nature of cataract severity.`r`nMSE Reduction & Clinical Safety: A critical contribution of our method is the drastic reduction in MSE to 0.42. In the context of ordinal regression, MSE reflects the distance of the error. An MSE of 0.42 implies an average prediction deviation of 0.42≈0.65 grades. This indicates a Bounded Error property: even when CLIP-CAT misclassifies a sample, the prediction typically lands on the immediate neighboring grade, rather than making catastrophic errors. This property is vital for clinical safety, ensuring that the model's decision logic aligns with the continuous physiological progression of the disease.
